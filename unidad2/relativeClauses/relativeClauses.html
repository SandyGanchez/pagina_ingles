Relative Clauses

Relative clauses are subordinate clauses that modify a noun or pronoun by using relative words such as who, which, whose, whom, where, and that.

01
BASIC CONCEPT

What Are Relative Clauses?

+

Relative clauses are subordinate clauses that modify a noun or pronoun.

They use relative words such as who, which, whose, whom, where, and that.

EXAMPLE

The woman who lives next door has two dogs.

FUNCTION

What Is Their Function?

Their function is similar to that of an adjective because they describe or specify a noun.

Relative clauses also help avoid the repetition of separate sentences.

EXAMPLE

The woman who lives next door has two dogs.

02 · RELATIVE WORDS

Relative Pronouns and Words

WHO
PEOPLE

Used to give information about a person.

The girl who is singing is my sister.
WHICH
THINGS

Used to give information about a thing.

The book which is on the table is mine.
WHOSE
POSSESSION

Used to show that something belongs to someone.

The boy whose bike is red is my friend.
WHOM
PEOPLE AS OBJECTS

Used formally for people when they function as the object of the clause.

The man whom she married is very kind.
WHERE
PLACES

Used to give information about a place.

This is the school where I study .
THAT
PEOPLE / THINGS

Used to give information about people or things.

The car that we bought is very fast.
03 · TYPES

Types of Relative Clauses

A
TYPE ONE

Defining Relative Clauses

Defining relative clauses are essential to the meaning of the sentence.

They provide crucial information that identifies the noun or pronoun in question.

They identify which person or thing the antecedent refers to.

Tip: Commas are not used.
WHO · PEOPLE WHICH · THINGS THAT · PEOPLE / THINGS
I have a friend who lives in London.
The car that we bought is very fast.
B
TYPE TWO

Non-Defining Relative Clauses

Non-defining relative clauses provide additional information about a noun or pronoun.

This information is not essential to the main meaning of the sentence.

Tip: They are placed between commas.
WHO WHICH WHOSE
My mother, who is a teacher, loves to read.
My mother, who is in New York, is visiting next week.
04 · STRUCTURE

Relative Clause Structure

The teacher who taught me in high school is retiring this year.
PERSON The teacher
RELATIVE WORD who
VERB taught
COMPLEMENT me in high school
STRUCTURE

Person + Relative Word + Verb + Complement
